Six singles matches, #1 through #6. The home team plays its ladder honestly. The guest team may reorder. Test an order of your own, or let the page search all 720 of them for the best stack, and see what each scoring system does about it.

Six UTR ratings per team, #1 through #6. Commas or spaces both work.

Notes — the win probability table

This is the chance the higher-rated player wins. Find the row for the two players’ average rating, then the column for the gap between them. For example a 10.00 against a 9.00 has an average of 9.50 and a gap of 1.00, which lands between the 9.00 and 10.00 rows at about 88.5% — so the 9.00 player wins roughly 11.5% of the time.

These are estimates, not official UTR Sports numbers. Treat any cell as good to about 3 to 5 percentage points either way for a normal junior match, and looser than that for elite players, ratings that have gone stale, injuries, or an unfamiliar surface. Gaps wider than 2.0 are not in the table and get extended using the same curve (about 2.07 per UTR point).

Notice that the same gap is worth slightly less as you go down the table. A 1.00 gap is 89% for players averaging UTR 4.00, but 88% for players averaging UTR 12.00 — stronger juniors are packed closer together in real ability than their ratings suggest.
